Sufi remembrance

Dhikr

A quiet bead counter, with its sources.

Dhikr, ‘remembrance’, is the repetition of God’s names and short formulas of praise. This app is a calm tasbih counter and study companion: the phrases with their meaning and sources, and the stations of the path as the early Sufi masters described them.

Inside the app

What you can do

I

A ring of beads

Tap the ring for each repetition. A gentle pulse marks each bead and a stronger one the end of a set.

II

The remembrances

The tasbih of Fatima (33 · 33 · 34), the testimony, seeking forgiveness, the Name, or a free count. Every phrase is shown in Arabic with transliteration and meaning.

III

Stations of the path

The seven stations and ten states as al-Sarraj listed them, with short reflections.

IV

What is dhikr?

What the Qur’an, the hadith and the manuals of al-Qushayri, al-Ghazali and Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh say about remembrance, with Sufi orders and modern Sufism labelled separately.

History & boundaries

What comes from the record, and what is new

EVIDENCEFrom the historical record

  • The Qur’an calls believers to remember God often (13:28, 33:41–42); the hadith teach particular phrases and counts.
  • Classical manuals by al-Sarraj, al-Qushayri, al-Ghazali (Iḥyāʾ, Book 9) and Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh made remembrance central to the Sufi path.

MODERNMade for this app

  • The counter, haptics and timers are features written for this app.
  • The app is a companion, not a teacher, and makes no claims about effects.

Sources named in the app: Qur’an 13:28, 33:41–42, 2:152 · al-Sarraj, Kitāb al-Lumaʿ · al-Qushayri, Risāla · al-Ghazali, Iḥyāʾ · Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh, Miftāḥ al-falāḥ · Schimmel · Knysh

Dhikr is a contemporary contemplative experience for study and reflection. It does not promise contact, prediction, initiation, healing or any other outcome, and it is not medical or psychological advice.
