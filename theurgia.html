Late-antique Platonism

Theurgia

Rise through the seven spheres.

Late-antique Platonists read the Chaldean Oracles as revelation and practised theurgy, ‘divine work’. Theurgia is a guided ascent built from those texts: an orrery of the ancient cosmos that follows the soul outward from the Earth, sphere by sphere.

History & boundaries

What comes from the record, and what is new

EVIDENCEFrom the historical record

  • The Chaldean Oracles survive only in fragments quoted by later Platonists. Iamblichus defended theurgy; Proclus and the emperor Julian wrote in its spirit.
  • The Poimandres (Corpus Hermeticum I.25–26) and Macrobius (Commentary on the Dream of Scipio 1.12) describe the soul’s passage through the spheres.

MODERNMade for this app

  • The guided ascent is a modern exercise built from ancient texts, not a reconstruction of a Chaldean rite.
  • Modern scholarship (Lewy, Dodds, Majercik, Shaw, Johnston) is labelled as scholarship.

Sources named in the app: Chaldean Oracles, ed. Majercik · Iamblichus, De mysteriis · Proclus · Julian, Hymn to King Helios · Corpus Hermeticum I · Macrobius

Theurgia is a contemporary contemplative experience for study and reflection. It does not promise contact, prediction, initiation, healing or any other outcome, and it is not medical or psychological advice.
