A seal for this hour

Made for this moment

The Ars Paulina tells its reader to make each angel’s seal “according to the time of the years Day and hour”, and gives examples drawn for the 10th of March 1641. This seal follows that instruction in a modern way: the angel’s name is traced across a rose of the alphabet, and the ring records this hour and this day. It is a new design, not the manuscript’s figure.

    The Heptameron

    The seven angels of the days

    The Heptameron, a short handbook printed in 1559 under the name of Peter of Abano, gives each day of the week an angel, a planet, companions, a heaven, a king of the air with his ministers, a wind and a perfume. Agrippa’s table of seven gives a slightly different list, noted under each angel.

    The Ars Paulina

    The twenty-four angels of the hours

    The Ars Paulina is the third book of the Lemegeton, compiled in the seventeenth century; its text mentions the year 1641. Its first part names an angel for each hour of the day, counted from sunrise, and of the night, with a court of dukes and servants. Peterson shows that much of it is drawn from the Steganographia of Trithemius. The general rule is set down at the start: “{{RULE}}” The names below follow British Library Sloane MS 3825; readings from the other manuscripts are in brackets.

    Sources and method

    How this page is made

    The hours are counted from sunrise and sunset calculated for your place, the day divided into twelve and the night into twelve. The planet of each hour follows the Chaldean order from the planet of the day. The angels of the hours are those of the Ars Paulina; the angels of the days and the angels of the four quarters are those of the Heptameron. The positions of the planets are calculated from their orbital elements and agree with professional ephemerides to within a small fraction of a degree.

    The names, hours, numbers and quotations come from the sources listed. The figures of the angels and the seals are modern devotional artwork made for Discovery Science and are not reproductions of any manuscript. This page describes a tradition; it does not claim that the angels or the hours influence events.
