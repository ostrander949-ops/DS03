Find your birth angel.

Enter your birth date to find which of the 72 angels of the Shem ha-Mephorash was keeping the Sun’s path when you were born, the angel of your birth day, and your own sigil traced on your planet’s magic square, after Agrippa.

The tradition

The 72 angels and your birthday

Three verses of Exodus (14:19–21) each have exactly 72 Hebrew letters. Read together in a particular pattern they give 72 three-letter names, the Shem ha-Mephorash, the “explicit name” of God. Adding -iah or -el to each gives the names of 72 angels. In the French tradition of the 18th and 19th centuries the 72 were spread around the zodiac, five degrees each, starting from the first degree of Aries at the spring equinox, and grouped eight to a choir through the nine choirs of angels, from the Seraphim to the Angels.

Your birth angel is the one whose five degrees the Sun was crossing on the day you were born. The angel of your birth day comes from an older book, the Heptameron, which gives each day of the week to an archangel and a planet. Your sigil follows Agrippa: your name traced as a line across the magic square of that planet. The live Angel of the Hour clock shows when each planet’s hours fall today.

Questions

What is a birth angel?

In the tradition of the 72 angels of the Shem ha-Mephorash, each angel is set over five degrees of the zodiac. Your birth angel is the one whose five degrees the Sun was passing through when you were born.

Where do the 72 angels come from?

Their names are drawn from three verses of Exodus (14:19–21), each 72 letters long, read in a pattern that yields 72 three-letter names. To each the suffix -iah or -el is added. The assignment to five-degree spans and to the nine choirs is the French tradition of the 18th and 19th centuries.

Why does my birth time matter?

The Sun moves about one degree a day, so for most people the date is enough. If you were born on the day the Sun crossed from one five-degree span to the next, your birth time decides which angel is yours.

How is the sigil made?

Following Agrippa (De occulta philosophia, Book II, chapter 22), each letter of your name is given the number of the Hebrew letter it stands for, reduced to fit the magic square of the planet that ruled the day you were born, and a line is drawn from number to number across the square.
