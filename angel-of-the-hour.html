The Ars Paulina tells its reader to make each angel’s seal “according to the time of the years Day and hour”, and gives examples drawn for the 10th of March 1641. This seal follows that instruction in a modern way: the angel’s name is traced across a rose of the alphabet, and the ring records this hour and this day. The next angels of the hours

    The Heptameron

    The seven angels of the days

    The Heptameron, a short handbook printed in 1559 under the name of Peter of Abano, gives each day of the week an angel, a planet, companions, a heaven, a king of the air with his ministers, a wind and a perfume. Agrippa’s table of seven gives a slightly different list, noted under each angel.

    ☉ Sunday · angel of the Sun

    Michael

    Michael is one of only two angels named in the Hebrew Bible. In the Book of Daniel he is “one of the chief princes” (10:13) and “the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people” (12:1). The New Testament calls him “Michael the archangel”, disputing with the devil over the body of Moses (Jude 9), and in Revelation “Michael and his angels fought against the dragon” (12:7). His name is a question: who is like God?

    The Heptameron makes him the angel of Sunday and of the Sun. On his day he rules with two companions, Dardiel and Huratapel; the spirits of the air are under a king called Varcan and his ministers Tus, Andas and Cynabal, all subject to the North wind; and his heaven is Machen, the fourth. The book prescribes a perfume for the day, printed in the 1655 English as “red wheat” and corrected by Peterson to red sanders.

    How the angels of the Sun appear

    Honorius describes the angels of the Sun with bodies “great and large, and full of all benevolence”, their colours “bright or citrus, or like the Sun or gold”. Agrippa’s Fourth Book gives the emblems of the Sun: a king with a sceptre riding on a lion, a crowned king, a queen with a sceptre, a lion, a cock, a golden garment, a sceptre.

    Agrippa Agrippa’s “scale of the number seven” gives the Sun to Raphael and moves Michael to Mercury.

    Ars Paulina In the second part of the Ars Paulina, Michael governs the spirits of the fiery signs “towards the South”, to be observed on Sunday in the first and eighth hours of the day and the third and tenth of the night. They are said to appear “in Royal Robes holding scepters in their hands, oft Ryding on a Lion or a Cock.”

    ☽ Monday · angel of the Moon

    Gabriel

    Gabriel is the other angel named in the Hebrew Bible. He explains Daniel’s visions (Daniel 8:16; 9:21), and in the Gospel of Luke he announces two births: “I am Gabriel, that stand in the presence of God” (Luke 1:19), and then to Mary in Nazareth (Luke 1:26). In Islam, as Jibril, he brings the revelation to Muhammad.

    In the Heptameron he is the angel of Monday and the Moon, ruling with Michael and Samael. The spirits of the air are under King Arcan and his ministers Bilet, Missabu and Abuzaba, subject to the West wind; his heaven is Shamain, the first and lowest, the heaven of the Moon. The perfume of the day is aloes.

    How the angels of the Moon appear

    Honorius describes the angels of the Moon as “long and large”, their forms “dark and white like crystal or of a burnished sword, or like ice or dark clouds”. Agrippa’s Fourth Book gives the emblems of the Moon: a king like an archer riding on a doe, a little boy, a huntress with bow and arrows, a doe, a goose, a green or silver garment, an arrow.

    Agrippa Agrippa agrees: Gabriel is the angel of the Moon.

    Ars Paulina In the second part of the Ars Paulina, Gabriel governs the spirits of the watery signs “towards the north”, with three mighty princes under him: Samael, Madiel and Mael. They are observed on Monday at the first and eighth hours of the day and the third and tenth of the night.

    ♂ Tuesday · angel of Mars

    Samael

    Samael does not appear in the Bible. He comes from later Jewish literature, where he is a great prince of heaven associated with the accuser and with death; the midrash Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer tells of Samael in the Garden of Eden. The grimoires keep the fierceness but put him to work as the angel of Mars.

    The Heptameron makes him the angel of Tuesday, ruling with Satael and Amabiel. The spirits of the air are under King Samax and his ministers Carmax, Ismoli and Paffran, subject to the East wind; his heaven is Machon, the fifth. The perfume of the day is pepper.

    What the old books say he can do

    StrengthVictory in conflictHealth given or taken

    Force and victory in conflict, armed help, and power over illness. In the old books Mars is the planet of war, and its hours were the most feared.

    “To cause wars, mortality, death and combustions; and to give two thousand Souldiers at a time; to bring death, infirmities or health.”

    Heptameron, on the spirits of Tuesday

    “Their nature is to provoke wars, murder, destruction, and mortality of people.”

    Sworn Book of Honorius, on the angels of Mars

    How the angels of Mars appear

    Honorius describes the angels of Mars as “of medium stature, dry and thin”, their colour “red, such as red-hot coals kindled well”. Agrippa’s Fourth Book gives the emblems of Mars: an armed king riding on a wolf, an armed man, a woman holding a shield, a he-goat, a horse, a raven, a red garment.

    Agrippa Agrippa gives Mars to Camael (כמאל) instead.

    Ars Paulina Samael is everywhere in the Ars Paulina: the manuscript names him (as “Samuel”, corrected to Samael) the angel of the first hour of every day, and in its second part he is one of the three princes under Gabriel.

    ☿ Wednesday · angel of Mercury

    Raphael

    Raphael is the healer. In the Book of Tobit he travels in disguise with young Tobias, binds a demon and cures his father’s blindness, then reveals himself: “I am Raphael, one of the seven holy angels” (Tobit 12:15). The Book of Enoch lists him among the archangels and has him bind the fallen Azazel (1 Enoch 10).

    The Heptameron makes him the angel of Wednesday and Mercury, ruling with Miel and Seraphiel. The spirits of the air are under King Mediat, or Modiat, and his ministers Suquinos and Sallales, subject to the Southwest wind; his heaven is Raquie, the second. The perfume of the day is mastick.

    KnowledgeProphecyJusticeHealing

    Learning and lost knowledge recovered, foreknowledge, favour with judges, healing, the poor raised up, and locked things opened.

    “To give all Metals; to reveal all earthly things past, present and to come; to pacifie judges, to give victories in war, to re-edifie, and teach experiments and all decayed Sciences, and to change bodies mixt of Elements conditionally out of one into another: to give infirmities or health; to raise the poor, and cast down the high ones; to binde or lose Spirits; to open locks or bolts.”

    How the angels of Mercury appear

    Honorius describes the angels of Mercury as “changeable, clear like glass, or like a flame of white fire”. Agrippa’s Fourth Book gives the emblems of Mercury: a king riding on a bear, a fair youth, a woman holding a distaff, a dog, a magpie, a garment of changeable colours, a rod, a little staff.

    Agrippa Agrippa’s scale swaps him with Michael: Raphael for the Sun, Michael for Mercury.

    Ars Paulina In the second part of the Ars Paulina, Raphael is the “sovereign prince” of the spirits of the airy signs, to be observed “towards the east” on Wednesday, with two princes under him, Miel and Seraphiel, the same two companions the Heptameron gives him.

    ♃ Thursday · angel of Jupiter

    Sachiel

    Sachiel is a grimoire angel: he is not in the Bible, and his name has come down in several spellings. The printed Heptameron reads Sachiel; Peterson records the forms Sadkiel and Satquiel in other manuscripts. Agrippa’s table of seven gives Jupiter to Zadkiel, and the two are now usually treated as one angel of Jupiter, the planet of kingship, law and abundance.

    The Heptameron makes him the angel of Thursday, ruling with Castiel and Asasiel. The spirits of the air are under King Suth and his ministers Maguth and Gutrix, subject to the South wind; his heaven is Zebul, the sixth. For the four quarters on Thursday the book gives prayers instead of angels, because “there are no Angels ruling the Air, above the fifth heaven.” The perfume of the day is saffron.

    How the angels of Jupiter appear

    Honorius describes the angels of Jupiter as “of medium stature”, their colour “like the color of the sky, or of crystal”. Agrippa’s Fourth Book gives the emblems of Jupiter: a king with a drawn sword riding on a stag, a man wearing a mitre in long raiment, a maiden crowned with laurel and flowers, a bull, a stag, a peacock, an azure garment, a sword.

    Agrippa Agrippa names Jupiter’s angel Zadkiel (צדקיאל). Peterson notes that other manuscripts of the Heptameron read Sadkiel and Satquiel, which is why Sachiel and Zadkiel are so often treated as one angel.

    Ars Paulina In the second part of the Ars Paulina, Sachiel is one of the three princes under Uriel, who governs the spirits of the earthy signs towards the West.

    ♀ Friday · angel of Venus

    Anael

    Anael is the angel of Venus, of love, beauty and friendship in the old correspondences. Like Sachiel he is a figure of the magical literature rather than the Bible; Agrippa gives the form Haniel. He is one of the few angels who appears in both the Heptameron and the Ars Paulina, ruling a day in one and an hour in the other.

    The Heptameron makes him the angel of Friday, ruling with Rachiel and Sachiel. The spirits of the air are under King Sarabotres and his ministers Amabiel, Aba, Abalidoth and Flaef, subject to the West wind; his heaven is Sagun, the third. The perfume of the day is pepperwort.

    ♄ Saturday · angel of Saturn

    Cassiel

    Cassiel is the angel of Saturn, the slowest and most distant of the seven, the planet of time, limits and solitude. A marginal note in the printed text adds “or Caphriel”, and other manuscripts read Caphael, Cafriel or Capciel.

    The Heptameron makes him the angel of Saturday, ruling with Machatan and Uriel. The spirits of the air are under King Maymon and his ministers Abumalith, Assaibi and Balidet, subject to the Southwest wind. No heaven is named for Saturday, and for the four quarters the book refers back to the prayers of Thursday. The perfume of the day is sulphur.

    EndingsBindingSeverity

    In the old books Saturn’s work is the hardest: discord, grief, cold and endings. Later astrology softened him into the planet of time, patience, discipline and limits.

    How the angels of Saturn appear

    Honorius describes the angels of Saturn as “long and slender, pale or yellow”. Agrippa’s Fourth Book gives the emblems of Saturn: a bearded king riding on a dragon, an old man with a beard, an old woman leaning on a staff, a dragon, an owl, a black garment, a sickle, a juniper tree.

    Agrippa Agrippa gives Saturn to Zaphkiel (צפקיאל).

    Ars Paulina In the second part of the Ars Paulina, Cassiel is one of the three princes under Uriel, governing the spirits of the earthy signs towards the West, observed on Saturday.

    The Ars Paulina

    The twenty-four angels of the hours

    The Ars Paulina is the third book of the Lemegeton, compiled in the seventeenth century; its text mentions the year 1641. Its first part names an angel for each hour of the day, counted from sunrise, and of the night, with a court of dukes and servants. Peterson shows that much of it is drawn from the Steganographia of Trithemius. The general rule is set down at the start: “The Nature of these 24 Angells of the day and night changeth every day: and their offices is to doe all things that are attributed to the 7 planetts. But that changeth every day also.” The names below follow British Library Sloane MS 3825; readings from the other manuscripts are in brackets.

    Ars Paulina: A Book of Hours · Google Play

    I · the first hour of the day

    Samael

    Ruler of the first hour of every day, the hour called Barim. In the manuscript he has many dukes and servants; 8 chief dukes named, with 444 servants apiece.

    Sloane 3825 spells him “Samuel”, corrected to Samael. The name of the hour is not given in the manuscript; “Barim” is supplied from Wolfgang Heidel.

    What he can do, in theory

    Like every angel of the hours, Samael has no planet of his own. The Ars Paulina says the offices of these angels change with the day: in his hour he does “all things that are attributed to” whichever planet rules it. So what the old books would ask of him depends on the day. Over a week he serves all seven:

    Sources and method

    How this page is made

    The hours are counted from sunrise and sunset calculated for your place, the day divided into twelve and the night into twelve. The planet of each hour follows the Chaldean order from the planet of the day. The angels of the hours are those of the Ars Paulina; the angels of the days and the angels of the four quarters are those of the Heptameron. The positions of the planets are calculated from their orbital elements and agree with professional ephemerides to within a small fraction of a degree.

    The names, hours, numbers and quotations come from the sources listed. The painted portraits of the angels are modern devotional artwork, made for Discovery Science with an AI image generator (Canva) from descriptions drawn from these texts: the colours and bodies the Sworn Book of Honorius gives each planet’s angels, the emblems in Agrippa’s Fourth Book, and the hours of the Ars Paulina. The gilded frames and the seals are drawn in code. None of it reproduces a manuscript figure. This page describes a tradition; it does not claim that the angels or the hours influence events.

    Questions

    Angel of the hour: common questions

    What is the Pauline angel of the hour?
    The Ars Paulina, the third book of the seventeenth-century Lemegeton, names an angel for each of the twelve hours of the day and the twelve hours of the night, counted from sunrise. The first hour of every day is ruled by Samael, the first hour of every night by Sabrathan.
    How is the Pauline hour different from the planetary hour?
    The planetary hour belongs to one of the seven planets and changes with the day of the week. The Pauline angel belongs to the hour’s number and is the same every day. The Ars Paulina joins the two: the angel of the hour does “all things that are attributed to” the planet that rules that hour.
    Which angel rules today?
    In the Heptameron each day has an angel: Michael on Sunday, Gabriel on Monday, Samael on Tuesday, Raphael on Wednesday, Sachiel on Thursday, Anael on Friday and Cassiel on Saturday.
    When does the angel of the day rule the hour too?
    In the first and eighth hours of the day and the third and tenth hours of the night, the planet of the hour is the planet of the day. These are the hours the Ars Paulina names for its observances on Sunday, Monday, Wednesday and Saturday.
    Is my location sent anywhere?
    No. Sunrise, sunset, the hours and the positions of the planets are calculated in your browser.
    Is the artwork historical?
    No. The painted portraits are modern devotional artwork made for Discovery Science with an AI image generator (Canva), from descriptions taken from the old texts: the colours and bodies the Sworn Book of Honorius gives each planet’s angels and the emblems in Agrippa’s Fourth Book. The frames and seals are drawn in code. The names, hours, numbers and texts come from the sources cited.
