I · What you hear

Identify the state. Catch the anomaly.

The EEG editions are built for two jobs: telling you, while your eyes are closed, which state you are in, and catching the rare events worth writing down.

The theta tone

You hear the threshold

When theta power rises above alpha and beta and holds there, a soft sustained tone enters the soundscape. It fades as you drift back up. With eyes closed, you learn to find the theta-dominant state and stay in it.

The gamma chime

You hear the burst

Each sudden surge of gamma power far above your own baseline, measured in standard deviations, is marked by a distinct chime and time-stamped, so you can match it to what you were experiencing.

III · How it works

The closed loop, in plain terms

Think of learning to balance on a bicycle. Nobody can explain the muscles involved, but the bike tells you instantly when you tip, and soon your body knows. Neurofeedback does the same for states of consciousness.

I

Listen

Dry electrodes pick up the brain’s rhythms, signals measured in millionths of a volt, 250 to 256 times a second.

II

Measure

Several times a second the app computes the strength of each rhythm and compares it with your baseline.

III

Answer

Within about a quarter of a second, the sound and the rite respond: the tone, the chime, the gate.

IV

Learn

States that are signalled become easier to find again. Over sessions you learn to enter them on purpose.

The method is sixty years old. In the 1960s Joe Kamiya showed that people could learn to recognise and then control their own alpha rhythm when given a signal for it, and Barry Sterman’s laboratory trained a 12–15 Hz rhythm in cats that, unexpectedly, made them resistant to seizures. Meditation research since has drawn a consistent picture: practice brings rising alpha and theta, expert meditators show increased frontal midline theta during concentration, and some long-term practitioners generate striking high-amplitude gamma. Studies of neurofeedback during meditation have found that people can learn to raise their theta power, and that real feedback moves the trained signal more than sham feedback does.

Pair that with binaural entrainment and you have the complete instrument: the sound invites the brain toward theta, and the EEG tells you, honestly and in real time, whether it went there.
