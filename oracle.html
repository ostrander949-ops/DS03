The ceremony

Every reading is a small rite

ORACLE does not throw a card at you. It slows the moment down, the way the traditions did.

I · Turn the card

Face down, then revealed

Your card waits face down at the centre of a slowly turning gold astrolabe. Hold the question, tap, and it turns over. Then the name, its themes and the reflection unfold in order.

II · Cast the coins

The I Ching, line by line

Three coins are thrown six times and the hexagram builds from the bottom up, as the Ten Wings describe. Changing lines glow gold and resolve into the second, relating hexagram.

III · The Synthesis

Four systems, one question

Draw a tarot card, a rune, a hexagram and a planet together. ORACLE compares their themes on the device and shows where they agree and where they pull against each other.

Scholar Mode

Built on the record, not on guesswork

Tarot was born as a card game in the courts of fifteenth-century Italy; the hand-painted Visconti-Sforza trumps survive in New York and Bergamo. It became an instrument of divination only in eighteenth-century Paris, with Court de Gébelin in 1781 and Etteilla, and took its modern face from the Rider-Waite-Smith deck of December 1909. ORACLE’s seventy-eight cards carry that whole history, so you always know which layer you are reading.

The Zhou yi is older still, a Western Zhou manual of sixty-four hexagrams whose core probably took shape in the late ninth century BCE. The classical yarrow-stalk method and the later three-coin method give the same overall chance of a changing line, one in four, but divide it differently between yin and yang.
