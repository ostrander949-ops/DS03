    How it works

    How the current hour is found

    The clock calculates sunrise and sunset for your place and date, divides the daylight into twelve equal parts and the night into twelve more, and gives the first hour after sunrise to the planet of the day: the Sun on Sunday, the Moon on Monday, Mars on Tuesday, Mercury on Wednesday, Jupiter on Thursday, Venus on Friday and Saturn on Saturday. Each following hour takes the next planet in the Chaldean order, Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, the Sun, Venus, Mercury and the Moon. The planetary day runs from sunrise to the next sunrise, so the small hours after midnight still belong to the previous day’s night.

    The angel shown is the angel the Heptameron, first printed in 1559, gives to the planet that rules the hour. The planets, the hours and the angels are a historical tradition; this page shows how that tradition reckons time and makes no claim that the hours influence events. The full history, worked examples and weekday tables are in the planetary hours guide.

    Questions

    Planetary hour now: common questions

    What planetary hour is it now?
    It depends on where you are, because planetary hours are measured from your local sunrise and sunset. This page calculates the current hour, its planet and its angel for your location and updates every second.
    Which angel rules the current planetary hour?
    In the tradition of the Heptameron each planet has an angel: Michael for the Sun, Gabriel for the Moon, Samael for Mars, Raphael for Mercury, Sachiel for Jupiter, Anael for Venus and Cassiel for Saturn. The angel shown is the angel of the planet that rules the hour.
    Why is this hour longer or shorter than sixty minutes?
    Planetary hours are unequal. Daylight is divided into twelve and the night into twelve, so day-hours are long in summer and short in winter. They are close to sixty minutes only near the equinoxes.
    Is my location sent anywhere?
    No. Sunrise, sunset and the hours are calculated in your browser. If you share your location, it is used on your device only.
    Why does another planetary hour site show slightly different times?
    Calculators define sunrise differently, for example by the upper edge or the centre of the Sun, with or without refraction. That moves every hour boundary by a minute or two.
