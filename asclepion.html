Asclepion

In the sanctuaries of Asclepius the petitioner purified, made an offering, and slept in the abaton, waiting for the god to come in a dream. At Epidauros the temple recorded the visits and the cures on stone. This recreation follows that pattern as a six-stage evening rite and a morning record.

The recreation

Evening and morning.

  • I · The evening rite. Six stages, with the screen darkening at each one. Sound is optional and fades out or stays off.
  • II · The morning report. Events, figures, speech, and the feeling on waking, with a vividness rating. Each night is kept as a stele in a private log.
  • III · The inscribed cures. Paraphrased accounts from the Epidauros stelae, read beside the ancient authors.
  • IV · Sources kept apart. Every statement is tagged as ancient source, late-antique reception, modern reception, modern exercise, or modern scholarship.
The record

What survives.

The Epidauros inscriptions (IG IV² 1, 121–124) record petitioners' dreams and the cures that followed. Pausanias and Aristophanes describe the sanctuary and the night spent in the abaton. Aelius Aristides kept a dream diary across years of illness, and Artemidorus set out how such dreams were read.

The six-stage rite is a modern exercise built on that pattern. It does not reconstruct a temple liturgy.

Sources: IG IV² 1, 121–124; Pausanias 2.26–27; Aristophanes, Wealth; Aelius Aristides, Sacred Tales; Artemidorus, Oneirocritica; Edelstein & Edelstein (1945); LiDonnici (1995); Renberg (2017).
